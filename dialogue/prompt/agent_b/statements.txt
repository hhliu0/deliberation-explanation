s1: Even if AI systems come to do most research and development, technological progress will stay gradual rather than becoming more than ten times faster within five years.
s2: By 2044, advanced AI systems will likely have goals that conflict with human interests in ways humans cannot reliably correct.
s3: Reducing the risk of human extinction from AI should be a global priority alongside pandemics and nuclear war.
s4: AI is worsening social problems such as misinformation, discrimination and economic inequality.
s5: If it applied equally to all developers, global AI progress over the next five years should be slower than it is now.
s6: Advanced AI should not be developed unless humans can reliably keep meaningful control over it, including the ability to override and shut it down.
s7: Concentrating advanced AI development in a few tightly controlled organizations is preferable to wide availability, because it makes risks easier to manage.
s8: AI companies should be left to manage risks to users, such as manipulation, addiction and harm to children, through their own safety practices rather than face legal liability and independent oversight.
s9: AI should be designed to empower people and protect their liberty, privacy and agency, not to curtail or replace them.
s10: The public's concerns about potential future AI risks, such as loss of control or extinction, largely stem from misunderstandings of AI.
s11: Society should devote more attention, discussion and resources to reducing AI's current risks and harms than to potential future existential or catastrophic risks. (3 = both deserve about equal focus)
s12: The direction and pace of AI progress are driven mainly by competitive and technological forces, not by human choices.
